# Trilingual Kids Learning Channel: Project Plan

Faceless kids' language-learning channel (English, French, Spanish) with three recurring cartoon characters.
Prepared 8 Oct 2026. Sections marked **(decision needed)** are still open.

---

## 1. Idea in one paragraph
Short, repetitive vocabulary videos for young children (about 3-6 years old): fruits, vegetables, animals, colors, numbers, seasons, nature, body, family, greetings. Each lesson is hosted by three cartoon friends. Each friend speaks one language, wears an accessory that signals it, and has a fixed voice and subtitle color. Audience on screen: kids. Audience in the descriptions: parents and teachers.

## 2. What the research said (Nexlev + web, all figures are estimates)
- No channel found doing exactly English + French + Spanish for kids. That is either a gap or low demand; I could not tell from a small sample.
- Kids/language content gets views but earns very little from ads:
  - Endless Productions (kids numbers, Spanish words): 7.9K subs, 3.9M total views, est. about $129/mo, monetized.
  - Mael Effect (kids songs): 17.9M views, est. about $130/mo.
  - Language-learning channels with 130K+ subs showed only about $50-$200/mo.
- Shorts: only close match was Raising Readers (English pronunciation, 46.7K subs, 65.5M views, monetized). Estimated Shorts revenue works out to about $0.10 per 1,000 views, so Shorts = discovery, not income.
- Animal and fruit Shorts in Spanish and French get millions of views (Animalandia ES, Curioscopio, Aaron - Animaux), so those topics have audience.
- Plan the money elsewhere: printables (flashcards, worksheets), possibly picture books. Pinterest is a good home for printables.
- The influencer video we analyzed (@grundstrombiz, 252K views) promotes the Nexlev tool and a "guide". Its $63,972/mo claim is unproven and was not used.

## 4. Voices
- Three distinct voices, one per character.
- **Bonbon (French):** native French, cheerful, warm, young-sounding female voice; clear and slow enough for a 4-year-old; no baby talk.
- **Churro (Spanish):** warm, friendly, slightly deeper. (decision needed on gender)
- **Milo (English):** bright, upbeat. (decision needed on gender)
- Do not clone a real child's voice or any real person's voice without permission.
- Check commercial-use rights for the plan you buy (YouTube monetization).
- Candidate tools (from web search, mostly vendor pages): ElevenLabs, Google Cloud TTS, HeyGen. Test the same script on two or three and listen for French and Spanish accent quality.
- I could not generate audio from the cloud session (no voice tool available, Microsoft speech service blocked).

## 5. Video 1 script: Fruits in 3 languages (about 5 minutes)
Name below is a placeholder; replace MILO with the final penguin name.

**Intro (about 15 s)**
- MILO: "Hello, friends! I'm Milo!"
- BONBON: "Bonjour, les amis ! Je suis Bonbon !"
- CHURRO: "¡Hola, amigos! ¡Soy Churro!"
- MILO: "Today we learn fruits in three languages!"

**For each fruit (about 13 s):** show the fruit scene big, with each word on screen in the speaker's color.
1. MILO says the English word (red).
2. BONBON says the French word (blue).
3. CHURRO says the Spanish word (yellow).
4. All three repeat it together, slowly.

| # | Fruit (MILO, English) | BONBON (French) | CHURRO (Spanish) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | apple | la pomme | la manzana |
| 2 | banana | la banane | el plátano (or la banana) |
| 3 | strawberry | la fraise | la fresa |
| 4 | orange | l'orange | la naranja |
| 5 | grapes | le raisin | las uvas |
| 6 | pear | la poire | la pera |
| 7 | watermelon | la pastèque | la sandía |
| 8 | lemon | le citron | el limón |
| 9 | cherry | la cerise | la cereza |
| 10 | pineapple | l'ananas | la piña |

**Quiz (after fruits 5 and 10, about 15 s each):** characters look puzzled and cover one fruit. MILO asks "What is it?", then a 3-second pause, then each character reveals the word in turn.

**Recap chant (about 25 s):** fast rhythm, all ten fruits, each character taking their own language.

**Outro (about 10 s)**
- MILO: "Great job! See you next time!"
- BONBON: "Bravo ! À bientôt !"
- CHURRO: "¡Muy bien! ¡Hasta pronto!"

**Before publishing:** native speaker check of French and Spanish (articles and accents); decide between plátano and banana (Spain vs Latin America); listen for odd pronunciation in l'ananas and la pastèque.

### Shorts version (20-40 s)
Question hook ("How do you say apple in 3 languages?"), three reveals, a mini quiz, loop back to the start.

## 8. Risks and cautions
- Ad income from kids' content is low. Plan for printables and other income.
- Check YouTube's current rules for "made for kids" content (comments and personalized ads are limited) and for repetitive or mass-produced content, plus disclosure for realistic AI-generated media.
- Big established kids' channels dominate; they are not visible in the Nexlev sample.
- AI voices make mistakes with French and Spanish articles and accents. Use a native check.
- Do not use real children's footage.
- Nexlev, Windsor and Canva numbers and images are estimates or AI output, unverified on the platforms.
